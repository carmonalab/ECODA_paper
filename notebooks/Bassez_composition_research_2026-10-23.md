# Bassez baseline composition: source-grounded interpretation

Research date: 2026-10-23. Scope: original BioKey/Bassez study, ECODA preprint v1, and the exact local Bassez cohort definition. **This is a literature/source investigation, not a new analysis of expression or abundance.** Observations below are attributed either to the publications or explicitly to the coordinating agent’s existing-artifact analysis; proposed explanations are marked **Hypothesis** or **Inference**.

## Bottom line

**Snapshot-specific caveat:** the coordinating agent’s existing HR artifact excludes unsubtyped populations (including cancer, fibroblasts and B cells), whereas whole-sample pseudobulk includes them. Therefore the observed contrast is not a clean test of annotation granularity alone: **cellular-compartment restriction also changes**. The current source documents a filling/repair step, so the older artifact must be distinguished from future repaired runs. Source details and parent-reported measurements are separated in §7.

**Matched-cell follow-up:** the all-cell comparison in §10 now resolves that scope concern for this snapshot: mixed-resolution author composition retains its advantage over PB HVG2000 when both represent all 74,948 cells.

The strongest source-supported explanation is **fine-grained immune-state composition**, not simply more immune cells: baseline experienced/exhaustion-like CD4/CD8 T-cell populations, migratory/immunoregulatory DCs and particular macrophage states are associated with subsequent TCR clonotype expansion. The original paper also demonstrates baseline *within-cell-type expression* differences, so a claim that expression is uninformative would contradict its findings. Whole-tumor pseudobulk can mix these state-specific signals with cancer-cell, stromal and other expression variation; whether that mechanism explains the actual benchmark gap requires the local loading and sensitivity analyses, not literature alone. [Bassez, Results: “T-cells expressing PD1 proliferate…”, “Expression signatures…”, “Immune environment…”; Figs. 1, 4–6][B-full]

**E/NE are expansion labels, not demonstrated clinical responder/non-responder labels.** Bassez explicitly states that this short window-of-opportunity study could not establish whether T-cell expansion translates into clinical benefit. The ECODA preprint’s response terminology in Fig. 2B/Supplementary Fig. 4 should therefore be qualified when interpreting this dataset. [Bassez, Discussion][B-full]; [ECODA, Fig. 2B and Supplementary Fig. 4][E]

A weak Leiden-resolution-2 result would not refute this biology: numerical resolution does not guarantee the same cell-state partition as curated, lineage-specific subclustering. More importantly, the coordinating agent reports that resolution 2 actually has slightly **higher ANOSIM** than author HR in the existing local bundle; the superiority claim is metric-dependent, not universal. [ECODA, Methods: “Cell type annotation”][E]; local evidence status in §7.

## 1. Exact cohort and endpoint

### Local cohort contract

The local [`datasets.json`, Bassez entry](../datasets.json) specifies breast tumor, input `BassezA_2021_33958794whole.rds`, sample identifier `sample`, label `expansion`, low-resolution annotation `cellType`, high-resolution annotation `cellSubType`. Its **benchmark_analysis** view keeps only `timepoint == "Pre"`, `expansion != "n/a"`, and `cohort == "treatment_naive"`. It is enabled for benchmarking, not the batch-effect benchmark. Thus the question is about **pre-treatment samples from treatment-naive patients with an available downstream expansion label**, not mixed pre/on-treatment samples or the chemotherapy-pretreated replication cohort. The JSON does not itself establish retained sample counts or independent-patient counts. [Local `datasets.json`, lines 59–83](../datasets.json)

### What expansion means

Bassez defines a clonotype using shared TCR sequences. A clonotype expands when its frequency or proportion increases on-treatment versus pre-treatment and its on-treatment frequency is >2 cells. The number of such expanded clonotypes must be **>30 per patient** to define expansion. A stricter definition requires increasing frequency and >5 cells on-treatment, with a threshold of 10 expanded clonotypes; it identifies the same expander group. Newly detected on-treatment clonal sequences can also count as expanded. For DEG/pathway comparisons of expanding cells, the authors use increased *proportion* and on-treatment frequency >2. Proportion is frequency normalized by cells with a detected TCR, not whole-tumor cellularity. [Bassez, Methods: “Clonotype expansion and contraction”, author-manuscript PDF p. 23][B-full]

The treatment-naive analyzed cohort comprised **29 paired patients: 9 E and 20 NE**. NE means limited or no clonotype expansion, not no immune infiltrate, no tumor killing, or proven treatment resistance. Eleven NEs had relatively high baseline T-cell fractions, whereas nine were T-cell-cold; total T-cell abundance alone therefore did not identify expansion. [Bassez, Results: “Single-cell profiling before and during anti-PD1 treatment”, PDF p. 4; Fig. 1c][B-full]

The study administered one 200-mg pembrolizumab dose before surgery; sampled needle biopsy before treatment and surgical resection 7–15 days later (reported as approximately 9 ± 2 days). A separate analyzed cohort of 11 patients had chemotherapy before anti-PD1 and contained 3 E/8 NE; it is excluded by the local benchmark filter. The protocol is single-center, open-label, non-randomized phase 0, with biomarker rather than clinical efficacy objectives. [Bassez, Methods: “Patient population and clinical study”, PDF pp. 17–18][B-full]; [Supplementary Protocol §§2–3][B-supp]

**Clinical-response warning:** the original Discussion says the window setting prevented testing clinical benefit. Reduced cancer-cell proportion on-treatment and immune activation are suggestive observations, not validation of E as pathological complete response, RECIST response, event-free survival, or treatment benefit. Even the reported scRNA-seq reduction in cancer-cell MKI67 was not confirmed by Ki67 immunohistochemistry. [Bassez, Results: “Cancer cells are affected…” and Discussion, PDF pp. 10–13][B-full]

## 3. Composition versus pseudobulk: what is actually supported?

**Direct publication evidence:** E has more baseline T cells and fewer fibroblasts; more informative still are TEX subtype proportions and PD1-associated programs. The paper reports AUCs of approximately 0.92–0.96 for PD1/TEX abundance/clonality/richness, versus approximately 0.57–0.76 for TIL scores, T-cell number or tumor mutation burden. This supports fine-state composition over a simple immune-infiltration explanation, but does not rank the current benchmark implementations. [Bassez, Results “T-cells expressing PD1 proliferate…”, Fig. 1 and Extended Data Fig. 3j][B-full]

**Direct ECODA evidence:** the preprint reports that author-HR CLR composition had the best *average* separation and that global pseudobulk was also a strong baseline. The per-cell-type pseudobulk representation had worse aggregate performance than global pseudobulk, which the authors interpret as evidence for a compositional contribution. This is multi-cohort evidence, not a mechanistic proof specific to Bassez; missing cell types, low cell counts and averaging distances across shared cell types also differ from global pseudobulk. [ECODA, Results following Supplementary Fig. 16; Methods “Pseudobulk per cell type”][E]

**Inference / mixture mechanism:** before nonlinear normalization, a sample-average expression can be written as a sum of cell-type proportions times their cell-type-specific mean expression. Different state mixtures can give similar global averages, and opposite changes can cancel. Composition explicitly preserves state counts and discards within-state expression; global pseudobulk mixes both. The benchmark pseudobulk is actually **summed raw counts, DESeq2 median-ratio size factors, variance-stabilizing transformation, and 2,000 pseudobulk HVGs**, not simply the arithmetic mean of log-normalized single cells. RNA-content and normalization effects mean a naive cell-weighted average is explanatory intuition, not an exact formula for the processed benchmark. [ECODA, Methods “Sample representation methods configuration”][E]

**Hypothesis — dilution/subtype competition:** localized TEX, DC or macrophage programs may be less salient in whole-tumor expression than variation in other lineages, tumor subtype or patient-specific tumor programs. This is plausible because Bassez reports patient-specific malignant clusters, shared non-malignant states, and receptor-subtype-dependent immune expression; it is not demonstrated merely by better ECODA separation. [Bassez, Results “Single-cell profiling…” and “Expression signatures…”][B-full]

**Not supported without local measurements:** that the discriminating genes are lowly expressed; that their sample variance is low; that they were excluded from HVG selection; that an immunoglobulin-rich PC is necessarily technical noise; that fewer genes or removing immunoglobulins improves separation; that subtype completely explains the pseudobulk structure; or that composition “removes all batch effects.” The preprint’s batch examples concern other cohorts, and local Bassez configuration excludes it from that benchmark. [ECODA, Fig. 5 and Methods][E]; [local configuration](../datasets.json)

The original paper’s baseline DEG/program findings also rule out the stronger assertion that E/NE biology is *only* abundance and never within-type expression. Cell-state boundaries themselves are expression-defined, so “composition versus expression” is a representation contrast, not two independent biological causes. [Bassez, Figs. 3–5 and corresponding Results][B-full]

## 4. Why Leiden resolution 2 need not contradict the subtype explanation

Bassez’s author labels were obtained by **lineage-specific** subclustering, not one uniform whole-tumor cut: T cells used Seurat integration and regression for patient, UMI count, mitochondrial fraction, cell cycle and IFN-response score; DCs were separated and re-clustered with pDCs. Proliferating T cells were further reassigned. Supplementary Fig. 1 explicitly shows that without cell-cycle/IFN regression, clusters mixed several T-cell phenotypes. The ECODA preprint describes an alternative nearest-neighbor/Leiden workflow across resolution values 0.1, 0.4, 2, 5, 20. Consequently, “2” does not imply equal granularity or recovery of exactly TEX, mregDC and macrophage contrasts. [Bassez, Methods “scRNA-seq clustering leading cell subtypes”, PDF pp. 19–20][B-full]; [Supplementary Fig. 1][B-supp]; [ECODA, Methods “Cell type annotation”][E]

**Hypotheses to distinguish:** resolution-2 clusters could merge relevant states, split a relevant state into sparse subclusters, preserve an orthogonal expression program, or over-partition patient-specific cancer cells. These are alternatives to test with an author-HR × Leiden contingency table, lineage-specific purity/recall, per-cluster sample prevalence, and sample-composition loadings. A good or bad separation score alone does not identify which partition occurred. The preprint itself reports broadly comparable Leiden performance on average and dataset-specific exceptions, including Bassez in Supplementary Fig. 19; these statements are compatible with local annotation-dependent differences. [ECODA, Results “ECODA is robust…”; Fig. 3, Supplementary Figs. 18–19][E]

## 5. Sample structure and confounding

- **Paired longitudinal source, baseline-only benchmark:** original observations include both timepoints, while local input filters retain only Pre. Downstream E/NE labels legitimately use the paired on-treatment TCR result; on-treatment cells must not enter a claim about baseline predictive representation. Independently verify sample-to-patient correspondence before treating all samples as independent patients. [Bassez, Fig. 1a][B-fig1]; [local configuration](../datasets.json)
- **Selection/capture:** 54 patients consented; the analyzed paired scRNA/scTCR cohorts contain 29+11. Exclusions included refused biopsy, incompatible 3′ profiling, insufficient cells, <100 retained cells, barcode mismatches, and suspected scar/necrotic sampling. Supplementary Table 1 distinguishes 31 treatment-naive transcriptomic participants from 29 expansion-classifiable patients; P1 failed scTCR and P15 had a suspicious fibroblast increase. ECODA additionally retains samples with at least 500 cells after its own QC. Thus publication enrollment, initial transcriptomic data and final benchmark counts are different denominators. [Bassez, Methods “Patient population…”][B-full]; [Supplementary Table 1 legend][B-supp]; [ECODA, Methods “scRNA-seq data pre-processing”][E]
- **Biopsy/site heterogeneity:** baseline needle core versus on-treatment resection samples different regions. The authors discuss random sampling bias for contracting clonotypes, and explicitly exclude a possible scar-tissue sample. Dissociation/capture fractions are not unbiased absolute tissue counts. [Bassez, Results “Single-cell profiling…”; Methods exclusions][B-full]
- **Receptor subtype:** treatment-naive expansion occurs in TNBC and ER-positive tumors, with no significant difference in expanded-clonotype number between the small E subgroups (5 TNBC versus 3 ER+). However, TNBC E has higher baseline PD1, CD4 TH1 and checkpoint programs; ER+ E shows relatively more naive/pre-effector markers. Therefore subtype cannot be assumed identical to E/NE, but remains a plausible driver of orthogonal pseudobulk variance. The hormone-receptor-positive protocol subgroup was selected for baseline TILs, limiting generalizability. [Bassez, Fig. 4d–f and Results “Expression signatures…”][B-full][B-fig4]; [Supplementary Protocol §2.1][B-supp]
- **Other clinical variables:** Supplementary Tables 1–2 record age category, histology, receptor status, stage, sampling interval, TILs, Ki67 and menopausal state. Their existence does not establish that they confound local PCs. Tumor mutation burden/chromosomal instability did not differ between E and NE in the authors’ analysis; lack of significance in this small cohort is not proof of no association. [B-supp]; [Bassez, Results “T-cells expressing PD1 proliferate…” and Extended Data Fig. 2g–h][B-full]
- **Technical expression variation:** study scRNA/TCR profiling used 10x 5′ libraries; gene-expression sequencing used NextSeq and/or NovaSeq, with TCR sequencing on HiSeq4000. The existence of several instruments does not prove instrument confounding in the retained baseline subset. Original QC/regression and the newer benchmark preprocessing are distinct operations. [Bassez, Methods “Single-cell RNA-seq and TCR-seq”, “Single-cell gene expression analysis”][B-full]; [ECODA Methods][E]
- **Metadata linkage is constrained:** the authors’ current data page says public patient IDs were randomized and do **not** match Supplementary Table 1 numbers. Clinical table rows must not be joined to public IDs by their numeric appearance. The current page describes patient, timepoint, treatment, expansion and hormone-receptor metadata and a linked TCR/barcode release. [Author data page, “Data availability”][B-data]

## 6. Targeted, falsifiable follow-through (not executed here)

1. **Composition/state mechanism:** quantify each author-HR feature’s PC1/PC2 contribution and E–NE CLR difference; test whether TEX-high versus CX3CR1-macrophage-high balances align with separation. Separate all-cell denominators from within-T-cell/within-myeloid balances; check whether the effect persists after controlling broad T-cell fraction. Prediction: a subtype balance retains information beyond total immune abundance. Basis: Figs. 1h, 5l, 6e. [B-full]
2. **Dilution rather than absent genes:** intersect published markers/signatures with the actual pseudobulk HVGs and report mean expression, variance, group association, PC loadings and cell-type specificity separately. Prediction: signature availability can coexist with PCs dominated by other variation. No conclusion about low expression or low variance follows from weak loading alone. Basis: original baseline signatures and benchmark HVG/PCA definitions. [B-full][E]
3. **Tumor subtype/purity hypothesis:** color the same PB PCs by receptor subtype and tumor/fibroblast/B-cell/T-cell fractions; evaluate within-subtype E/NE distances with patient-level inference and small-sample caution. Compare immune-only and lineage-restricted representations against the same cohort. Prediction: if tumor mixture/subtype masks immune-state signal, restricting lineage reduces that competing variance. Do not equate improved supervised separation after post-hoc choices with independent validation. [B-full][E]
4. **Leiden mapping hypothesis:** cross-tabulate author states against res-2 and res-20 clusters, including marker programs and per-sample counts. Prediction: the explanation must identify actual merging/splitting or irrelevant partitions, not rely on “resolution 2 is low.” [B-supp][E]
5. **Sensitivity/endpoint robustness:** assess missing/zero-count subtypes, counts per sample and zero replacement; stratify by high versus low baseline T-cell content; compare continuous expanded-clonotype association when available. Predictions must distinguish capture depth from relative abundance and avoid treating E/NE as clinical response. [B-full][E]

Neither literature retrieval nor the JSON establishes current per-sample clinical covariates, sequencing-batch assignments, expression mean/variance, or author-to-Leiden mapping. Those facts remain **unmeasured in this research slice**, not established negatives. No datasets, pipeline/HPC scripts, existing artifacts, or configuration were modified. No verification gates or formatters were run.

- PB unit-variance scaling yields **0.2347621**, and excluding **128 immunoglobulin genes** yields **0.2186503**, versus approximately **0.31456** baseline PB ANOSIM. Neither intervention improves this snapshot. Ig genes account for approximately **24.30%** of feature variance, **39.49%** of PC1 contribution and **7.69%** of PC2 contribution in the exported sensitivity table; dominance and usefulness are not mutually exclusive.

All these sensitivity statistics are explicitly exploratory and use **zero permutations**; no significance claim follows from them. [Sensitivity output](../plots/Bassez_diagnostics_2026-10-23/20261023T141110/Bassez_sensitivity.csv)
